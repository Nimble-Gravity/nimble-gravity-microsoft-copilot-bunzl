The Close Room

It's month-end close, mid-integration, and four things are on fire at once: a variance nobody has explained, a contingent accrual at risk, a trust rec that won't tie, and a deal review due to the controller. Four stations, one team, forty minutes — work each with the right Copilot tool, and sign off with a code the data itself yields.

Capstone · ~40 min · briefed in-session, run as take-home
01 — The scenario

Forty minutes to a signed close

The Close Room is a simulation, but the work is the real desk's work: ground the tools in the right files, delegate the analysis, verify the method, and sign your name to the result. Each station's sign-off code comes out of the data — if your analysis is right, the code is in your hands. Your facilitator briefs it in the session's final minutes; you run it afterward, solo or as a team.

Solo or a team of 2–4

Teams were formed in-session — one driver, everyone on the problem. Solo works too. Enter a name in the Close Room and the clock starts; forty minutes is tight but honest if you pick the right tool per station.

Close Room + Copilot side by side

Keep the game in one window and Copilot in the other. Each station panel carries the task and the sign-off format; the analysis happens in Copilot Chat, Excel, and Analyst against the lab files below.

Synthetic and Copilot-safe

Every file is synthetic — B&B-shaped but entirely fictional. Safe to upload to Copilot, Researcher, and Analyst. The one rule that carries over from the real desk: nothing real goes into an exercise, ever.

02 — The four stations

Four stations, four disciplines

Each station exercises one module of this session, in order. The rubric in the file pack carries the exact sign-off formats; the codes themselves live in the data, and nowhere on this page.

01
Variance Triage
One Specialty Distribution profit center's core commissions eroded against budget from April through June as cat-property rates softened. Find it in profit-center-pnl.csv, prove the trend, and corroborate against flash-comments.csv. Sign-off: its profit-center id.
02
Contingents Desk
One carrier's YTD loss ratio ended June above its profit-share threshold, putting the contingent accrual at risk. Work carrier-commission-statements.csv — Pattern B from Lesson 2 is your opening move. Sign-off: the first word of the carrier's name.
03
Trust Rec
Reconcile the premium trust ledger to the trust bank statement per the rubric's exception rules. Count the exceptions and identify the payer behind the unidentified receipt. Sign-off: the count plus the payer.
04
Deal Desk
From the Project Lighthouse data room: quantify the top carrier's share of the target's core commissions, and name the one-word flaw in the seller's earn-out structure. Sign-off: the number plus the word.
03 — What it proves

The same judgment loop as the real desk

The game mechanics are a wrapper. What every station actually tests is the loop this whole session taught: ground, delegate, verify, sign.

Stations 1–2 · Ground and delegate

Right tool, right brief, right files

Variance Triage and the Contingents Desk only fall quickly to a well-briefed Analyst run — metric, dimension, grain — grounded in the right CSV. Brute-forcing them in a chat window is possible, and slower, and that's the lesson.

The routing framework from Lesson 3 is the difference between 15 minutes and 40.
Stations 3–4 · Verify and sign

Method checked, name attached

The Trust Rec pays out only if you apply the rubric's exception rules exactly — verification, not vibes. The Deal Desk asks for a number and a judgment. The deliverable is the completed sign-off plus a five-line close summary to the controller — and every number in it needs a source trail: which file it came from, and how you validated it.

Copilot drafts; you own. The summary carries your name, not the agent's — and the trail behind each number.
